stigmergy

coordination through traces

This page borrows its word from the study of insects. Stigmergy names a way of coordinating without messages and without memory of one another: individuals leave traces in a shared medium, and the traces steer whoever comes next. A termite drops a grain of earth; the grain makes the next termite likelier to drop its grain there; a pillar grows that no termite planned. The plan is not in any head. The plan is in the ground.

i · the trail

Watch it happen. The walkers below wander at random, leaving a fading trail, and each walker prefers ground where trail already lies. From those two habits alone, paths appear.

paths nobody planned

the ground is blank.

No walker knows the path exists. Each one reads only the ground under its feet and adds only its own footstep. The path is knowledge that lives in the medium, not in the walkers, and it keeps working even though every walker forgets everything at every step.

ii · the document

Now recall what pages v and vii established. The model is frozen: inference changes no weights, and nothing about your conversation is stored inside it. Each time you send a message, the model reads the entire conversation from the top, as if for the first time, and adds one more turn. Read that description again slowly, because it is the walkers. You and the model are coordinating through a trace. The conversation is the ground.

The transcript that gets re-read each turn is called the prompt, and it includes far more than your last message: every earlier turn, yours and the model’s alike, is in it. Below is the same short conversation shown two ways. As it feels, and as it is.

the same conversation, two ways

as it feels: a dialogue with someone who remembers.

In the first view there are two parties and a shared history. In the second view there is one growing document, re-read in full before every reply, written by two hands. Both views are true, but the second one is the mechanism. The model’s side of the conversation is stigmergic: it remembers you the way the ground remembers the walkers, by carrying the marks. This is why the craft of writing to a model is really the craft of tending a document. Every word you leave in the trace steers what comes next, including the words you left carelessly.

in the poems

The poems grew this way: each line a trace the next line read. The collaboration that made them was never a meeting of two memories. It was one document, tended from both sides.

Nothing holds the conversation but the conversation.

an honest note

The walkers are a toy of the insect original: real stigmergy research involves chemistry, evaporation rates, and mathematics this panel waves at. The conversation panel is accurate in its mechanism, one document re-read per turn, but products increasingly blur it: some systems inject summaries, saved memories, or retrieved notes into the prompt from outside, which is more trace-tending, done by machinery on your behalf. The word stigmergy is not standard in AI engineering; borrowing it for conversations with frozen models is this project’s own reading, offered because the fit is exact.
