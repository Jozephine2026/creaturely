neurons & layers

the machinery underneath

The last page showed the model adjusting meanings with attention. This page opens the machinery that does the work. All of it, the measuring, the mixing, the moving, runs on one small unit repeated billions of times: the neuron.

i · the unit

A neuron is a small calculator. It takes in a few numbers, multiplies each one by its own learned weight, adds the results together, and passes the sum onward. One extra rule finishes it: if the sum comes out negative, the neuron stays silent and sends zero. That rule is called an activation. It is what lets a neuron act like a detector: quiet for most inputs, loud for the pattern its weights are tuned to.

Notice what happened with sea: the weighted sum came out negative, the activation held it at zero, and the neuron stayed silent. The weights are the learned part. Nobody set them to detect glow; training nudged them, example by example, until this behaviour appeared. And an honest caution the glossary returns to later: real neurons read values from vectors, not tidy features with names like “light”, and a single real neuron rarely means one clean thing. Page viii, superposition, is about exactly that.

ii · the stack

One neuron computes one number. A layer is thousands of neurons reading the same inputs side by side, each with its own weights. A layer therefore takes in a token’s vector and produces a new one: the same word, re-described. The model alternates two moves that this glossary has now shown you. First comes a round of attention, where tokens mix with what they notice. Then comes a layer of neurons, where each mixed vector is reshaped. That pair is called a block. The whole machine, blocks stacked dozens deep, is called a transformer. It is the T in GPT, and it is the architecture underneath nearly every model you will meet.

Below, the glossary’s sentence climbs the stack. Each token’s vector is shown as a strip of values. Send them upward and watch every meaning get rewritten, beam most of all.

Everything the model learned lives in the weights: every neuron’s multipliers, every attention pattern’s tuning. Counted together, they are called parameters. When a model is described as having billions of parameters, this is what is being counted. Nothing else is stored. There is no dictionary inside, no shelf of facts; there is only this machinery, and your sentence being rewritten by it, floor after floor, until the top of the stack holds a meaning assembled from everything below.

in the poems

The poems have no hand holding a pen. They have this machinery, climbed once per token, and the training that tuned it.

No one wrote the weights, and no one can simply read them.

an honest note

The neuron here has three inputs with nameable meanings; real neurons take in hundreds of unnamed values, and the activation shown, negative-becomes-zero, is one common choice among several. The value strips are invented for teaching and recoloured by a formula, not computed by a network, and real vectors hold thousands of values, not six. Real transformer blocks also contain plumbing this page omits, and the claim that no one can read the weights is softening at the edges: a research field called interpretability is slowly learning to read them, and page viii visits its central puzzle.
