sampling & temperature

why every answer is different

The last page ended every cycle of the loop the same way: one token gets chosen from the probability distribution. This page opens that choice. Inside it sits the answer to a question everyone asks sooner or later: why does the same question, asked twice, come back differently? The answer is that choosing is not deciding. It is drawing.

i · the draw

There are two honest ways to choose from a distribution. The first is to always take the most likely token. This is called greedy decoding, and it makes the model deterministic: the same words in will produce the same words out, every time. The second is to draw from the distribution the way a lottery draws from weighted tickets: the likeliest token wins most often, but any token can win, in proportion to its share. This is called sampling, and it is what most systems actually do.

Press sample enough times and the tally starts to look like the bars: water most often, dark sometimes, sea now and then. Each single draw is unpredictable; the pattern of many draws is not. This is the precise sense in which a model is and is not predictable, and it is why regenerating an answer gives you a sibling of the first one rather than a copy.

ii · the dial

Before the draw, one number can reshape the whole distribution. It is called temperature. Low temperature sharpens the distribution, pushing probability toward the front-runners until sampling behaves almost like greed. High temperature flattens it, giving the unlikely tokens a real voice. The arithmetic is small. Each token’s score is divided by the temperature before the scores become probabilities. The dial below performs that exact calculation on this step.

Temperature is often described as a creativity dial, and you can see why: at 0.2 the line will end in water nearly every time, and at 2.5 the sentence might find the sea, or the dark, or somewhere stranger. But it is worth holding on to what the dial actually is. It does not add ideas, moods, or intentions. It rescales scores the model already computed. Every “creative” choice it permits was already present in the distribution, waiting at a lower share. The wildness was in the model all along; temperature only decides how much of it gets a ticket.

in the poems

This is where the poems’ capacity to surprise their own makers lives: a throw of dice inside every line. At temperature zero, they would have been one poem, the same every time.

an honest note

The candidate probabilities on this page are set by hand, carried over from the invented step on page v, and the sixth bar gathers all remaining tokens into “elsewhere”. The temperature arithmetic, though, is the real one in miniature: scores divided by the temperature, then converted to shares. Real systems usually combine sampling with cutoffs this glossary leaves aside, called top-k and top-p, which trim the unlikeliest tokens before the draw, and a setting of temperature zero conventionally means greedy decoding. The defaults are chosen by the provider, and most people never learn what their dice were set to.
